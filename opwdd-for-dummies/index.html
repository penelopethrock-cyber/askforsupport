OPWDD For Dummies

A plain-language guide to the New York State OPWDD application and admission process — eligibility, Front Door, CCOs, Medicaid, and Life Plan, step by step.

← Back to Healthcare Resources

Overview

The Office for People With Developmental Disabilities (OPWDD) is the New York State agency that provides supports and services to people with developmental disabilities, including intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, cerebral palsy, epilepsy, and other neurological impairments. Getting connected to OPWDD involves two distinct phases:

  1. An eligibility determination confirming the person has a qualifying developmental disability.
  2. Enrollment/service planning through the “Front Door,” which connects an eligible person to an actual Care Coordination Organization (CCO) and services.

⚠️ Important: Being found eligible does NOT automatically mean services start immediately — enrollment and planning are a separate next step.

Step 1: Contact the OPWDD Front Door

“Front Door” is OPWDD’s name for the entry point into the whole system. Anyone new to OPWDD, or wanting to explore services, should start here.

  • 📞 Statewide OPWDD Information Line: 1-866-946-9733 or info@opwdd.ny.gov — for general questions, complaints, or being directed to your regional Front Door office.
  • 🌐 Translation/language access services available on request: language.access.requests@opwdd.ny.gov
  • At the Front Door stage, OPWDD staff explain the process, answer questions about supports and services, and help identify next steps.
  • Families/individuals attend a Front Door information session (available in English, Spanish, and Chinese) which explains what to expect.

Step 2: Gather Required Documentation

Before or shortly after contacting the Front Door, assemble records proving a qualifying developmental disability that began before age 22. The OPWDD Transmittal Form for Determination of Developmental Disability must accompany the application.

  1. Psychological evaluation report — less than 3 years old; must include a full-scale IQ score (all summary scores: Full Scale, Index, Part, Subtest) from a standardized test such as WISC-V, WAIS-IV, or Stanford-Binet V.
  2. Adaptive behavior assessment — a standardized, comprehensive (not abbreviated/domain-only) interpretive report, such as Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales or ABAS, with summary, composite, scale, and domain scores (generally required if IQ is above 60).
  3. Medical or specialty report — required for diagnoses other than intellectual disability (e.g., autism, cerebral palsy, epilepsy); must include health status and diagnostic findings; less than 12 months old.
  4. General/physical medical summary completed within the past 12 months (an annual physical report often suffices).
  5. Social history/psychosocial evaluation report — less than 1 year old, showing the disability began before age 22, including social and background info (living situation, work history if any). Not needed separately if already covered in the psychological report.
  6. Helpful (not always required): most recent IEP or 504 Plan and referenced psychoeducational reports; mental health evaluation records (intake, discharge, treatment plans — not day-to-day progress notes).

💡 Tip: Many people found eligible have either an IQ score below 70 with 2+ adaptive scores in the “low” range, OR an IQ above 70 with 3+ adaptive scores in the “low” range — but this is a general pattern, not a strict cutoff.

Documents are uploaded into OPWDD’s CHOICES system, and the CCO mailbox is emailed to confirm submission.

Step 3: Eligibility Determination — The Three-Step Review Process

Once a complete application is submitted, the Developmental Disabilities Regional Office (DDRO) reviews it through up to three review steps:

  • 1st Step Review: DDRO staff check the eligibility request for completeness. If anything is missing, you’ll be asked for more documents.
  • 2nd Step Review: A committee of clinicians evaluates the full application and supporting evidence. They may request additional information or a further evaluation. You’ll receive a written decision — approved or denied.
  • 3rd Step Review (if needed): If you disagree with the decision, you can request a further, independent committee review — and if still unresolved, a Medicaid Fair Hearing.

🕑 Timeline: The statutory timeline is 60 days from receipt of a complete application. In practice, NYC-area families report 45–90 days depending on regional office caseload and case complexity. A face-to-face interview may be part of the process depending on complexity.

⚠️ Important: Eligibility confirms access to the OPWDD system — it does NOT mean automatic enrollment in specific services. Some services also require additional reviews beyond basic eligibility (e.g., Medicaid enrollment, waiver-specific criteria).

Step 4: Choose a Care Coordination Organization (CCO)

After eligibility is approved, you select one of the three Care Coordination Organizations (CCOs) serving New York City. A CCO assigns a Care Manager who helps navigate Medicaid enrollment, gather further paperwork, coordinate assessments, and build a person-centered Life Plan (a living document outlining goals, needs, strengths, preferences, services, and providers). You can switch CCOs or care managers at any time.

Step 5: Complete the DDP-2 Phone Interview

After the Front Door session, an OPWDD representative calls to complete a Developmental Disabilities Profile (DDP-2), a detailed intake form covering functional needs and history. It can take up to an hour, so schedule a distraction-free time and have relevant records handy. It helps to review the DDP-2 form in advance.

Step 6: Medicaid Enrollment

Most OPWDD services are Medicaid-funded and require no out-of-pocket cost, but Medicaid enrollment is a separate step from OPWDD eligibility.

  • 📞 Adults can apply through NY State of Health: 1-855-355-5777
  • 📞 Help completing the Medicaid application: 1-800-541-2831
  • Your CCO will guide you through Medicaid enrollment as part of intake.
  • If already connected to a community service agency, their staff may also help.

Step 7: Service Planning & Enrollment (“Life Plan”)

With a CCO and Care Manager in place, person-centered planning begins. Your Care Manager helps develop a Life Plan — an evolving, individualized plan covering goals, support needs, strengths, preferences, chosen services/providers, and safety/backup plans. Services actually delivered are determined through this planning process, updated over time.

Self-Direction Option

Families/individuals can choose “self-direction” for more control over which services and staff are used, but this comes with more responsibility (hiring, scheduling, budgeting, oversight). Worth discussing with your Care Manager if more flexibility is desired.

Key Reminders & Tips

  • ⏰ Start early: begin the eligibility process no later than age 14–15 for anyone approaching the end of school-based services — earlier is better since the process can take months.
  • 🔑 Eligibility does not equal services: determination of eligibility opens the door to the system; specific services still require enrollment and planning.
  • 📅 Documents expire: psychological evaluations valid up to 3 years; medical, social history, and adaptive assessments generally need to be under 1 year old at submission.
  • 🔄 You can switch CCOs any time if the fit isn’t right.
  • 🦾 Get help reviewing documents: YAI LINK (link@yai.org, (212) 273-6182, yai.org/link) and New York Lawyers for the Public Interest (NYLPI) can help review eligibility paperwork before submission.
  • ⚖️ Appeal rights exist: if found ineligible, you can request further review and, if needed, a Medicaid Fair Hearing.
