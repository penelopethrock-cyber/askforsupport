Housing Resources

Affordable housing lotteries, tenant rights support, homelessness prevention, and disability-related housing programs in New York City.

Affordable Housing Lottery
NYC Housing Connect

NYC's official portal for applying to affordable housing lotteries across the five boroughs, run by the Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD). Create a profile once and apply to multiple listings as they open.

📞 Affordable Housing Lottery Helpline: (212) 863-7990 ✉️ nychousingconnect@hpd.nyc.gov

Tenant Rights
Met Council on Housing – Tenants' Rights Hotline

Free tenant advocacy hotline open to any tenant living in New York City. Volunteer-staffed, answers questions on repairs, harassment, rent stabilization, evictions, and connects tenants to organizing support and legal referrals.

📞 (212) 979-0611 🕒 Mon 1:30-8pm, Wed 1:30-8pm, Fri 1:30-5pm
🌐 Learn More
Tenant Rights
NYC Tenant Helpline

A one-stop city service to inform NYC tenants about their rights and connect them to housing-related resources, including free legal services. Reach it through 311.

📞 Call 311, ask for "Tenant Helpline"
🌐 Learn More
Homelessness Prevention
HRA Homebase

NYC's homelessness prevention program, offering case management, help resolving rent arrears, mediation with landlords, and connections to emergency financial assistance to help households stay housed or find new housing before entering shelter.

📞 DHS Main Number: (212) 361-8000 ☎️ Or dial 311 to find a Homebase location near you
🌐 Learn More
Disability Housing
Disability Rent Increase Exemption (DRIE)

A NYC program that freezes the rent for eligible low-income tenants with disabilities in rent-regulated apartments, exempting them from future rent increases. The landlord receives a property tax credit to offset the frozen rent.

📍 NYC Department of Finance 💰 Income and disability-status eligibility required
🌐 Learn More
Disability Housing
Section 811 Supportive Housing for Persons with Disabilities

A federal HUD program providing project-based rental assistance so very low-income adults with disabilities can live independently in the community, often paired with voluntary supportive services.

📍 Administered by state/local housing agencies
